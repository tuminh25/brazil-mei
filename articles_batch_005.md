# How to Check Your EZ-Link Card Balance in Singapore (SimplyGo Guide)

If you've ever tapped out at a fare gate and quietly hoped there was enough value left on your card, you already know why it's worth knowing exactly how to **check EZ-Link card balance** information properly. Since Singapore's public transport ticketing moved to the account-based SimplyGo system, the way commuters check their balance has changed significantly from the older card-only readers many residents grew up with. This guide walks through every reliable way to check your balance, review your transaction history, and avoid getting caught out with an empty card at the gate.

## Key takeaways

- The **SimplyGo app** is the most complete way to check your EZ-Link card balance, review transaction history, and manage top-ups from your phone.
- Not every EZ-Link card today works exactly like the old stored-value cards — some cards and payment methods now operate under SimplyGo's account-based system, which affects how and when your "balance" updates.
- You can also check your balance at **ticketing kiosks** and **Passenger Service Centres** located at MRT stations across the network.
- Setting up **auto top-up** through the SimplyGo app removes the need to manually check your balance before every trip.
- If a transaction or balance update looks delayed or wrong, there are specific steps to take before assuming something is broken.


## Why checking your EZ-Link balance works differently now

For many years, EZ-Link cards operated as simple stored-value cards — you topped up cash value onto the card itself, and every trip deducted directly from that stored balance, visible instantly at any card reader. That's still broadly true for many standalone EZ-Link cards used purely for transit.

However, Singapore's transport ticketing has moved toward **SimplyGo**, an account-based fare system. Under SimplyGo, some cards and payment methods (including certain bank cards and mobile wallets used for transit) are linked to an account rather than functioning as a pure prepaid stored-value card. Fares in these cases may be calculated and settled slightly differently, which is why relying solely on an old-style fare gate display isn't always the most accurate way to understand your current balance or recent transactions.

This doesn't mean every EZ-Link card has changed — many commuters still use a standard EZ-Link card with a stored balance exactly as before. But it does mean the **SimplyGo app** has become the single most reliable source of truth for your balance and transaction history, regardless of which type of card or payment method you're using, since it consolidates this information in one place.

## Method 1: Check your balance in the SimplyGo app

The SimplyGo app, available for both iOS and Android, is the primary tool Singapore's transport operators recommend for managing your EZ-Link cards and related payment methods. Once installed and set up, the app allows you to:

1. **Add your card** — link your physical EZ-Link card, concession card, or supported payment card to the app.
2. **View your current balance** — select the card from your list to see its balance and expiry date at a glance.
3. **Review transaction history** — see a full breakdown of past trips, including boarding and alighting points, fares charged, and dates.
4. **Manage multiple cards in one place** — if your household has several EZ-Link or concession cards, you can track them all from a single account.

To check your balance specifically: open the app, tap on the card you want to review, and your balance, expiry date, and recent transactions will be displayed on that card's detail screen.

This method is particularly useful because it doesn't require you to be near a station or kiosk — you can check from anywhere, at any time, which is especially handy before heading out for a trip when you're unsure whether you'll need to top up.

## Method 2: Check your balance at ticketing kiosks

If you don't have a smartphone handy, or simply prefer an in-person check, MRT stations across Singapore are equipped with several types of kiosks that can display your card balance:

- **Top-Up Kiosks** — primarily used for adding value to your card, but they also display your current balance before and after a top-up.
- **Assisted Service Kiosks** — general-purpose ticketing terminals that can show your balance and recent transaction details when you tap your card.
- **SimplyGo Kiosks** — dedicated terminals specifically designed to support SimplyGo-related account and balance checks.

To use any of these, simply tap your EZ-Link card on the designated reader, and your balance will appear on the kiosk's screen. This remains a dependable backup option, especially for commuters who don't use smartphones regularly or who want a quick check while already at a station.

## Method 3: Ask at a Passenger Service Centre

Most MRT stations have a staffed **Passenger Service Centre**, typically located near the fare gates. If you're unable to use a kiosk or the app — for example, if your phone battery has died or you're having trouble with the app — station staff can check your card balance and recent transaction history for you directly.

This option is slower than the app or a kiosk during peak hours, when queues can form, but it's a reliable fallback, particularly for elderly commuters or anyone less comfortable with self-service technology.

## Reviewing your transaction history

Beyond just checking your current balance, the SimplyGo app lets you review a detailed **transaction history** for each card. This is useful for:

- Confirming that a specific trip's fare was charged correctly
- Tracking your monthly transport spending
- Identifying any unusual or unexpected charges that need to be queried
- Keeping records for expense claims, if your workplace requires supporting documentation for transport claims

To access this, open the app, select the relevant card, and scroll through the list of past transactions, which typically includes the date, time, boarding/alighting points (for train trips), and the fare charged.

## Setting up auto top-up so you don't have to check manually

One of the most practical features within the SimplyGo app is **auto top-up**. Rather than remembering to check your balance regularly, you can automate the process entirely:

1. Open the SimplyGo app and select the EZ-Link card you want to set up.
2. Link a supported payment method, such as a bank card, to the app.
3. Choose your preferred trigger amount — the balance threshold at which a top-up should automatically occur.
4. Set your preferred top-up value — how much should be added each time the trigger is reached.
5. Confirm the setup, and the app will automatically top up your card whenever the balance falls below your chosen threshold.

Once set up, you don't need to actively check your balance before most trips, since the system handles replenishment in the background. This is especially useful for commuters who travel frequently during peak hours, when discovering an empty card at the gate is most inconvenient.

## What to do if your balance or transaction looks delayed or incorrect

Because SimplyGo processes some transactions with a short delay rather than instantly, it's not unusual for your balance or your most recent trip's fare to take a little time to appear correctly in the app. If something looks off:

1. **Wait and check again** — give it some time, as SimplyGo transactions can take a while to fully process and reflect in your account.
2. **Review your full transaction history** — sometimes a fare appears differently than expected due to how a trip was calculated (for example, distance-based fare adjustments), rather than an actual error.
3. **Check for app or system updates** — occasionally, delays are linked to temporary system processing rather than an issue with your specific card.
4. **Contact SimplyGo support** if the discrepancy persists after checking your transaction history and waiting a reasonable amount of time — they can investigate specific transactions on your account.

Avoid assuming a charge is wrong purely because it doesn't appear instantly; in most cases, waiting a short period and then reviewing the detailed transaction history resolves the confusion.

## Practical tips for managing your EZ-Link balance

- **Check your balance before a long trip**, particularly if you're heading somewhere with limited top-up facilities nearby.
- **Set a sensible auto top-up trigger amount** — too low, and you risk running out mid-journey; too high, and you might tie up more funds on the card than necessary.
- **Keep the SimplyGo app updated** to ensure you have access to the latest features and accurate balance displays.
- **Register your card** where possible, since registered cards can offer additional protections (such as balance retrieval) if the card is lost, compared to unregistered cards.


## Frequently asked questions

**What is the fastest way to check my EZ-Link card balance?**
The SimplyGo app is generally the fastest and most convenient method, showing your balance, expiry date, and transaction history in real time without needing to visit a station.

**Can I check my balance without a smartphone?**
Yes. Use a Top-Up Kiosk, Assisted Service Kiosk, or SimplyGo Kiosk at most MRT stations, or approach staff at a Passenger Service Centre for assistance.

**Why doesn't my balance reflect my most recent trip immediately?**
SimplyGo transactions can take some time to process fully. If your balance still looks incorrect after checking again later, review your full transaction history in the app or contact SimplyGo support.

**Do all EZ-Link cards work the same way under SimplyGo?**
Not exactly. While many cards still function similarly to traditional stored-value cards, some cards and linked payment methods operate under SimplyGo's account-based system, which can affect how quickly balances and transactions update. The SimplyGo app remains the most reliable way to check accurate, current information regardless of card type.

**How do I avoid running out of value on my card?**
Set up auto top-up in the SimplyGo app so your card is topped up automatically whenever it falls below a threshold you choose, removing the need for manual balance checks before most trips.

**Can I check the balance of a concession card the same way?**
Yes. The SimplyGo app supports adding and tracking EZ-Link cards as well as SimplyGo Concession Cards, including checking balance and reviewing transaction history for each.

**What should I do if I lose my EZ-Link card?**
If your card was registered, contact SimplyGo support to report it lost — registered cards typically offer more options for balance protection than unregistered ones. Unregistered cards generally cannot have their balance recovered if lost.

## Final practical takeaway

The SimplyGo app remains the most reliable and convenient way to check your EZ-Link card balance, review your transaction history in detail, and set up auto top-up so you're never caught short at the fare gate. Kiosks and Passenger Service Centres remain solid backup options when you don't have your phone on hand, and understanding that some cards now operate on an account-based system helps explain why balances and transactions may not always update instantly the way older stored-value cards once did.

# Income Tax Filing in Singapore (2026): Deadline, Steps and What You Need

Every year, a familiar wave of confusion hits Singapore residents around March and April: do I need to file, when exactly is the deadline, and what happens if I miss it? This guide covers everything you need to know about **income tax filing in Singapore for 2026**, based on IRAS's official Year of Assessment 2026 guidance, including the exact filing window, who's required to file, and how to complete the process correctly through the myTax Portal.

## Key takeaways

- For **Year of Assessment (YA) 2026** — which covers income you earned during the 2025 calendar year — individuals could file their Income Tax Return between **1 March and 18 April 2026**.
- The **individual filing deadline was 18 April 2026**. Missing this deadline can result in penalties or an unfavourable estimated assessment.
- Filing is done through the **myTax Portal** (mytax.iras.gov.sg), accessed using Singpass.
- Many taxpayers are covered under the **No-Filing Service (NFS)**, meaning they don't need to actively file if their income information has already been submitted by their employer and other reporting parties.
- **Corporate Income Tax** follows a completely separate filing deadline from individual tax — this is a common point of confusion for residents who also run a business or side hustle.


## Understanding Year of Assessment: 2026 versus income earned in 2025

One of the most common points of confusion in Singapore's tax system is the relationship between the **Year of Assessment (YA)** and the **year the income was actually earned**. Singapore taxes income on a preceding-year basis, meaning:

- **YA 2026** relates to income earned during the **2025 calendar year** (1 January to 31 December 2025).
- The **filing and payment obligations for YA 2026** occur in the following months, starting from March 2026.

So when you see "income tax filing Singapore 2026," it specifically refers to the filing exercise conducted in 2026 for income earned the year before. This distinction matters because your income for YA 2026 purposes is based entirely on your 2025 earnings, deductions, and circumstances — not anything that happened in 2026 itself.

## The 2026 filing window: 1 March to 18 April

IRAS confirmed that for YA 2026, individuals could file their Income Tax Return via myTax Portal **from 1 March 2026 through to the deadline of 18 April 2026**. This roughly seven-week window gives taxpayers time to gather documentation, review pre-filled information, and submit their return without last-minute pressure — provided you don't leave it until the final days.

Filing outside this window, particularly filing late, can result in penalties and potentially an estimated Notice of Assessment issued by IRAS based on their own calculation, which taxpayers would then need to formally dispute if it doesn't reflect their actual income accurately.

## Who actually needs to file

Not every taxpayer in Singapore needs to actively submit a tax return each year. IRAS operates a system where a significant number of taxpayers are automatically covered.

### The No-Filing Service (NFS)

IRAS confirmed that around **1 million taxpayers received their tax bill directly** from mid-March 2026, without needing to file manually. This happens under the **No-Filing Service**, which applies to eligible individuals whose income information has already been submitted directly to IRAS by their employers (under the Auto-Inclusion Scheme) and other approved organisations, such as banks reporting interest income in some cases.

If you're on NFS, IRAS effectively pre-assesses your tax based on the information already submitted, and you'll simply receive your tax bill without needing to log in and file separately — provided nothing else about your income situation requires disclosure.

### When you generally need to file

You are typically required to file your own Income Tax Return if any of the following apply:

- You received a specific notification from IRAS instructing you to file, even if you believe your income falls below the taxable threshold.
- You have income that isn't automatically reported to IRAS, such as freelance or self-employed income, rental income from property, or overseas income remitted into Singapore.
- You are a sole proprietor or otherwise self-employed.
- Your personal circumstances changed in a way that affects your reliefs, deductions, or overall tax position, and this hasn't already been reflected through automatic reporting.

If you're unsure whether you fall under NFS or need to actively file, the safest approach is to log in to the myTax Portal directly — it will clearly indicate your specific filing obligation for that Year of Assessment.

## Step-by-step: how to file your income tax return

If you do need to file, here's the practical process:

1. **Log in to myTax Portal** at mytax.iras.gov.sg using your Singpass credentials.
2. **Check your filing status** — the portal will show whether you have an active filing obligation or are already covered under the No-Filing Service.
3. **Review pre-filled information** — for most employees, salary and CPF-related information is auto-included based on your employer's submission under the Auto-Inclusion Scheme (AIS), so you won't need to manually enter these figures.
4. **Declare any additional income** not already reflected — this includes freelance work, rental income, or income earned overseas that was remitted into Singapore.
5. **Claim eligible tax reliefs and deductions** — common examples include CPF cash top-up relief, course fee relief, parent relief, and other reliefs you may qualify for based on your circumstances during 2025.
6. **Review your full return carefully** before submission, checking that all income sources and reliefs are accurately reflected.
7. **Submit your return** by clicking through the final confirmation step in myTax Portal, ideally well before the 18 April deadline rather than at the very last moment.
8. **Retain your Notice of Assessment** once it's issued, for your personal financial records and any future reference needs, such as loan applications.

## Common mistakes residents make

- **Assuming no notification means no obligation.** Notification methods and personal filing requirements can vary year to year — always check the myTax Portal directly rather than assuming your situation is unchanged from the previous year.
- **Forgetting to declare side income.** Freelance work, tuition income, rental income, or overseas remittances remain your responsibility to declare, even if your main employment income is automatically pre-filled.
- **Leaving filing to the last day.** Server load and last-minute technical issues are more likely to cause problems if you wait until 17 or 18 April to file.
- **Confusing personal and corporate tax deadlines.** If you also operate a business, note that **Corporate Income Tax** follows an entirely separate filing timeline from individual income tax — missing this distinction has led some self-employed residents to mistakenly believe they've already met their obligations when they haven't.


## Individual versus corporate tax deadlines: don't mix them up

This is a genuinely common area of confusion, particularly for residents who run a side business or company in addition to personal employment:


| Tax type | Applies to | Filing deadline (relevant Year of Assessment) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Individual Income Tax | Salaried employees, self-employed individuals, freelancers | 18 April (for the applicable filing window, e.g. YA 2026: 1 March – 18 April 2026) |
| Corporate Income Tax | Registered companies | Separate deadline; for YA 2026 this was 30 November 2026 |

If you're both a salaried employee and a company director or sole proprietor of a registered business, you may have **two separate filing obligations** — one for your personal income tax, and a completely separate one for your company's corporate tax — each with its own deadline and portal process.

## What happens if you miss the deadline

Missing the individual filing deadline can lead to:

- **Late filing penalties**, which can increase the longer the delay continues.
- **An estimated Notice of Assessment**, where IRAS calculates your tax based on their own estimate of your income rather than your actual figures — this estimate is not always favourable and may result in you paying more than necessary until you formally object and correct it.
- **Potential further enforcement action** for prolonged non-compliance, in more serious or repeated cases.

If you realise you've missed the deadline, the best course of action is to file as soon as possible and, if necessary, contact IRAS directly to explain the delay, rather than continuing to leave it unresolved.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is the deadline to file income tax in Singapore for 2026?**
Individuals and partnerships needed to file their Income Tax Return by **18 April 2026**, within a filing window that opened on 1 March 2026, covering income earned in 2025.

**Do I need to file if I only have one employer and no other income?**
Possibly not. If you're covered under the No-Filing Service and your employer has submitted your income details under the Auto-Inclusion Scheme, IRAS may issue your tax bill directly without requiring you to actively file. Always confirm your specific status on myTax Portal.

**What happens if I miss the filing deadline?**
You may face late filing penalties, and IRAS may issue an estimated Notice of Assessment based on their own calculation of your income, which you would then need to formally address if it's inaccurate.

**Is the corporate tax deadline the same as the individual deadline?**
No. Corporate Income Tax for YA 2026 had a separate deadline of 30 November 2026, distinct from the individual filing deadline of 18 April 2026. If you run a business, you likely have two separate filing obligations.

**How do I check whether I need to file this year?**
Log in to myTax Portal using your Singpass — it will clearly show whether you have an active filing obligation for the relevant Year of Assessment or are already covered under the No-Filing Service.

**Can I amend my tax return after submitting it?**
If you notice an error after filing, you can generally make amendments through myTax Portal or by contacting IRAS directly, though it's best to review your return carefully before the initial submission to avoid needing corrections later.

## Final practical takeaway

Don't assume you're automatically exempt from filing simply because you didn't receive a physical letter in the past — log in to myTax Portal early within the 1 March to 18 April 2026 window to confirm your specific obligation, declare any income that isn't already pre-filled, claim the reliefs you're entitled to, and submit well ahead of the deadline to avoid penalties or an unfavourable estimated assessment.

# How to Book a Polyclinic Appointment in Singapore (HealthHub, SingHealth, NUHS, NHG)

One of the most common sources of frustration for Singapore residents is discovering that **polyclinic appointment booking** isn't handled through a single universal app or website. Depending on which cluster manages your nearest polyclinic, you might need to use a different platform entirely. This guide breaks down exactly how appointment booking works across Singapore's three public healthcare clusters, so you can book, check, or manage your visit without wasting time on the wrong channel.

## Key takeaways

- Singapore's public healthcare system is organised into **three main clusters** — SingHealth, National Healthcare Group (NHG), and National University Health System (NUHS) — each managing its own network of polyclinics.
- **HealthHub** serves as a national health services platform that can help you view appointments and identify which cluster manages your nearest polyclinic.
- **SingHealth Polyclinics** can be booked via an online request form or by phone during specified operating hours.
- **NUHS** appointments are managed primarily through the **NUHS App**, with HealthHub's eServices as an alternative channel.
- **NHG** institutions, including their polyclinics, are managed through the **NHG Health App** or by contacting the specific institution directly.
- These booking channels are intended for **non-emergency, non-urgent** healthcare needs — genuine emergencies should go to a hospital's Emergency Department or call 995.


## Why there isn't just one polyclinic booking app

Singapore's public healthcare system is deliberately structured around three regional clusters, each responsible for managing hospitals and polyclinics within their coverage area. This structure supports coordinated care within each region but means there's no single, unified booking app that covers every polyclinic islandwide.


| Cluster | General coverage area | Primary booking channel |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| SingHealth | Eastern and central-eastern Singapore | Online request form, phone booking |
| National Healthcare Group (NHG) | Central and northern Singapore | NHG Health App, direct institution contact |
| National University Health System (NUHS) | Western Singapore | NUHS App, HealthHub eServices |

Because each cluster manages its own scheduling systems, the most efficient approach is to first identify which cluster your nearest polyclinic belongs to, and then go directly to that cluster's specific booking channel rather than searching generically.

## Step 1: Identify which cluster manages your polyclinic

Before attempting to book, you need to know which of the three clusters your nearest polyclinic falls under. There are a few reliable ways to check this:

1. **Search HealthHub** for your polyclinic by name — HealthHub listings typically indicate which cluster manages a given polyclinic, along with contact details and services offered.
2. **Check the specific polyclinic's own webpage** — most polyclinics list their cluster affiliation clearly in their "About Us" or "Contact" section.
3. **Call the polyclinic's general enquiry line** — staff can direct you to the correct booking channel if you're unsure which system to use.

Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons residents get frustrated trying to book — attempting to use an app or portal that doesn't cover their specific polyclinic wastes time and leads to unnecessary confusion.

## Step 2: Use the correct booking channel

### HealthHub — your starting point

**HealthHub** functions as a broader national health services platform, allowing residents to:

- View existing appointments across supported institutions
- Access lab reports
- Check immunisation records
- Use eServices for appointment management at institutions that support this integration, including NUHS

Importantly, HealthHub has noted that Singpass is **not required** for many of its core services, which makes it accessible for a quick check even if you'd prefer not to log in with your full digital identity credentials for a simple lookup.

### SingHealth Polyclinics

If your polyclinic falls under SingHealth, you have two main options:

1. **Online appointment request form** — available on the SingHealth Polyclinics website, allowing you to submit your request for a specific date, time, and reason for visit.
2. **Phone booking** — call the SingHealth Polyclinics appointment line directly. Typical operating hours for phone bookings are **Monday to Friday, 8.00am–4.30pm**, and **Saturday, 8.00am–12.30pm**, with the line closed on Sundays and public holidays.

Booking by phone can be useful if you need to discuss specific scheduling needs or have questions about which type of appointment slot you require, while the online form is generally faster for straightforward bookings.

### NUHS Polyclinics

For polyclinics managed under NUHS:

1. **Download and use the NUHS App** to make, change, or cancel your appointments directly from your phone.
2. **Alternatively, use HealthHub's eServices** (accessible via eservices.healthhub.sg/appointments) if you're unable to use the NUHS App for any reason.
3. Some specific NUHS polyclinics also allow you to log back into the NUHS App or HealthHub to check for newly available slots if your preferred time isn't initially available.

### NHG Polyclinics

For polyclinics under the National Healthcare Group:

1. **Use the NHG Health App**, which supports appointment-related services for NHG institutions, including polyclinics.
2. For enquiries not resolved through the app, **contact the specific NHG institution directly** using their published contact details.
3. Remember that these services are explicitly intended for **non-emergency, non-urgent cases**.

## Changing or cancelling an appointment

If your plans change after booking, the process generally mirrors how you made the original booking:

- **SingHealth Polyclinics** — contact via the same phone line or check whether the online system supports rescheduling requests directly.
- **NUHS Polyclinics** — use the NUHS App to change or cancel your appointment, or use HealthHub's eServices if the app isn't accessible.
- **NHG Polyclinics** — manage changes through the NHG Health App, or contact the institution directly if the app doesn't support the specific change you need.

It's good practice to cancel or reschedule as early as possible once you know you can't make an appointment, freeing up the slot for another patient and avoiding any no-show implications some clinics may apply for repeated missed appointments.

## Same-day appointments: what to realistically expect

Availability for same-day polyclinic appointments depends entirely on how full a specific polyclinic's schedule already is on that day — this is not something that can be guaranteed or predicted in advance through any booking channel. Some practical points to keep in mind:

- Booking channels typically display real-time slot availability, so you'll see immediately whether same-day slots remain open when you check.
- Morning slots tend to fill up faster than afternoon slots, particularly at busier polyclinics.
- If no same-day slots are available and your condition isn't urgent, you may need to book for the next available day, or consider a nearby polyclinic within the same cluster if your condition allows for some flexibility in location.
- For anything that feels urgent but isn't a genuine emergency, some polyclinics or clusters may offer walk-in or urgent-care pathways — check directly with the specific institution, since this varies by location and isn't guaranteed everywhere.


## Emergency versus non-emergency: know the difference

It's worth being explicit about this distinction, since polyclinic appointment systems are not designed for emergencies:

- **Polyclinic appointment booking channels** (HealthHub, SingHealth's system, NUHS App, NHG Health App) are all intended for **non-emergency, non-urgent** healthcare needs — routine check-ups, chronic disease management, minor ailments, and similar situations.
- **For genuine emergencies** — chest pain, severe injury, difficulty breathing, or other life-threatening situations — go directly to the nearest hospital's Emergency Department or call **995** for an ambulance. Do not attempt to book a polyclinic appointment for an emergency situation.


## Practical tips for a smoother booking experience

- **Identify your cluster first**, before trying any specific app or portal, to avoid wasted time on the wrong platform.
- **Use app-based channels where available** (NUHS App, NHG Health App) rather than relying solely on phone lines, which can have longer wait times during peak periods, especially Monday mornings.
- **Pre-register through HealthHub** roughly 30–90 minutes before your appointment where this feature is supported — this can help reduce your waiting time once you arrive at the polyclinic.
- **Keep your NRIC and Singpass details accessible** if the specific booking channel requires login for full appointment management functionality.
- **Book slightly in advance where possible**, rather than relying on same-day availability, particularly for routine or non-urgent visits.


## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need Singpass to book a polyclinic appointment?**
Not always. HealthHub has noted that Singpass is not required for many of its core services, though some cluster-specific apps or full appointment management features may require login for complete functionality.

**Which app should I use if I don't know my polyclinic's cluster?**
Start with HealthHub, which can help you identify which cluster manages your nearest polyclinic, and also supports appointment viewing and eServices for supported institutions, including NUHS.

**Can I book a same-day polyclinic appointment?**
It depends entirely on how full that specific polyclinic's schedule already is on the day you're checking — booking channels show real-time availability, but same-day slots are never guaranteed.

**What if the app or online system isn't working for my booking?**
Each cluster offers a phone-based or direct-contact alternative — SingHealth Polyclinics can be booked by phone, and NUHS or NHG institutions can be contacted directly if their respective apps aren't accessible.

**Are these booking channels suitable for emergencies?**
No. Polyclinic appointment booking channels are explicitly intended for non-emergency, non-urgent cases. For emergencies, go directly to a hospital's Emergency Department or call 995.

**Can I change my appointment after booking it?**
Yes, generally through the same channel you used to book — the NUHS App and NHG Health App both support managing existing appointments, and SingHealth Polyclinics can typically be contacted to reschedule.

## Final practical takeaway

Before attempting to book a polyclinic appointment, take a moment to confirm which of the three healthcare clusters — SingHealth, NHG, or NUHS — manages your nearest polyclinic, since each relies on a different primary booking channel. Starting your search with HealthHub is a reliable first step if you're unsure, and remember that all of these systems are built for non-emergency care — for anything urgent or life-threatening, head straight to an Emergency Department or call 995.

# MyCareersFuture Guide: How to Find Jobs and Get Noticed in Singapore

**MyCareersFuture** is Singapore's national job portal, developed and run by Workforce Singapore, built around a simple but underused idea: matching jobs to your actual skills rather than just keywords in a job title. Many residents treat it purely as another listings site, scrolling through postings without setting up their profile properly — and in doing so, miss out on features that can genuinely improve their job search.

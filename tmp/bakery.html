How to Start a Legal Home-Based Food Business in Singapore (2026 Rules)

How to Start a Legal Home-Based Food Business in Singapore (2026 Rules)

If you've searched "can I sell bread from my home" or "how to start a home bakery," the good news is that a legal home-based food business in Singapore is genuinely more accessible than most people assume — but it does come with specific rules from three different authorities that you need to follow correctly. This guide breaks down exactly what's required in 2026, based on official Singapore Food Agency (SFA) guidance and the HDB/URA Home-Based Business framework.

The Short Answer: Do You Need an SFA Licence?

No. Home-based food businesses currently do not require an SFA licence to operate. The Singapore Food Agency's own official guidance states this clearly: given their small scale of operations, home-based food businesses pose lower food safety risks compared to restaurants and other food establishments that do require an SFA licence.

This means there is no dedicated "SFA home-based food licence" application, and no licensing fee to pay purely for operating a small-scale home food business from your own residential kitchen. However — and this is the part many first-timers miss — you are still required to comply with specific food safety requirements set by SFA, even without a licence.

You only become subject to full SFA food establishment licensing once you move out of your home kitchen and into commercial premises — a rented central kitchen, a shopfront, a hawker stall, or a registered food factory. At that point, you are no longer operating as a "home-based" business, and standard licensing, inspections, and fees apply.

The Three Frameworks That Govern Home-Based Food Businesses

A legal home-based food business in Singapore sits at the intersection of three separate regulatory frameworks:

  1. HDB (for HDB flats) or URA (for private homes) — through the Home-Based Business Scheme, which governs whether you're allowed to run a small business from your residential address at all, and under what conditions.
  2. SFA — which governs food safety requirements specifically, even though no licence is required for genuinely home-based operations.
  3. ACRA — which governs business registration if you're operating under a business name or selling regularly with the intention of profit.

Step 1: Confirm Your Business Fits the Home-Based Business Scheme

Under the Home-Based Business Scheme (covering both HDB flats and, separately, private property under URA), several conditions apply:

  • The business must be carried out by people actually living in the flat or house — family members under the same roof are fine to help.
  • No employees from outside your household can work at your residence under this scheme.
  • No external signage is allowed advertising your business from the property.
  • Operations must not cause noise disturbances or strong smells that affect neighbours.
  • The business must remain small-scale and clearly secondary to the residential use of the property — it cannot effectively turn your home into a full commercial operation.

For most common small home-based businesses, including food businesses within the intended scale, you generally do not need to seek separate prior approval from HDB or URA before starting — the scheme is designed to allow small-scale operation without an approval application, as long as you comply with its conditions.

Step 2: Understand What SFA Food Safety Rules You Must Follow

Even without a licence requirement, SFA imposes food safety rules specifically for home-based food businesses, covering the following areas:

  1. Comply with general food laws. You must still meet baseline food safety standards even without formal licensing.
  2. No food catering. Home-based food businesses must not offer catering services — this is explicitly prohibited under the current framework.
  3. No selling to food retailers. You cannot supply your home-made food to other retailers for resale.
  4. No selling at or to temporary fairs. Home-based food businesses are not permitted to sell food at, or to, temporary fair events.
  5. No ready-to-eat raw seafood. This category is strictly prohibited for home-based operations due to elevated food safety risk.
  6. All ingredients must be legally sourced. You cannot use unlicensed or unverified ingredient suppliers.
  7. Prepared and sold food must be safe for consumption. This is the umbrella requirement underlying all the specific rules above.

Generally permitted product categories for home-based businesses include cakes, cookies, pastries, bread, and other baked goods, along with certain other low-risk food products. Higher-risk categories, such as ready-to-eat raw seafood or large-scale catering-style production, generally require a licensed commercial kitchen rather than a home kitchen — regardless of how small your current operation feels.

Because food safety regulations can be periodically updated, always check the latest SFA guidance before finalising your product range, rather than relying solely on older blog posts.

Step 3: Complete Food Safety Training

Most guidance for starting a home-based food business in 2026 recommends completing the WSQ Food Safety Course Level 1, which typically covers:

  • Personal hygiene practices
  • Cross-contamination prevention
  • Safe food storage temperatures
  • Cleaning and sanitisation procedures

This training directly supports your ability to meet SFA's food safety requirements in practice, even though there's no licence tied to it.

Step 4: Set Up Your Kitchen Properly

Before you start production, take these practical steps:

  • Deep-clean your kitchen thoroughly before beginning any commercial-scale food preparation.
  • Separate raw and cooked ingredients during preparation — never let raw meat or seafood cross paths with ready-to-eat items.
  • Ensure proper refrigeration and clear labelling of all ingredients, including allergen information on finished products.
  • Keep pets out of the kitchen during any food preparation activity.
  • Use dedicated equipment and utensils for your business — don't reuse the same chopping board for raw chicken and for cake decorating, for example.
  • Practise FIFO (First-In, First-Out) rotation for ingredients, and allocate dedicated shelves or storage areas specifically for business use, separate from your household's regular food storage.

Step 5: Decide Whether You Need to Register with ACRA

You are required to register your business with ACRA if you operate under a business name, sell products or services regularly, or otherwise intend to make a profit in a sustained, business-like manner. If you're simply doing one-off bakes for friends occasionally, this threshold may not apply yet — but once you start branding yourself, advertising online under a business name, or regularly selling to strangers rather than people you personally know, ACRA registration becomes required.

Registering is done through the ACRA BizFile+ portal using your Singpass. You'll choose a business name, pay the registration fee (typically around \$15 for the name application plus \$100 for sole proprietorship registration), and list your home address as your registered business address. You'll usually receive your Business Profile within a few hours to a few days after successful registration.

Step 6: Inform HDB or URA, If Required

For most small-scale home-based businesses operating under the general Home-Based Business Scheme, you do not need to separately inform HDB or URA. However, if your specific business activity falls under the related Home Office Scheme (a slightly different category covering certain home-based professional or office-type activities), you may need to register your home address with the relevant authority — done online through HDB's Home Office Scheme page for HDB flats, or through URA for private property. This step, when required, is free and relatively quick.

Step 7: Set Up an Ordering and Payment System

Keep your operations simple and professional:

  • Offer clear ordering channels (messaging apps, a simple order form, or social media).
  • Set fixed self-collection windows for customers, which helps control foot traffic at your residence and reduces disturbance to neighbours.
  • Establish clear payment terms upfront to avoid disputes.

Step 8: Keep Proper Records for Tax Purposes

Income earned from your home-based food business is taxable in Singapore, and IRAS expects you to declare it as part of your annual income. Keep basic, ongoing records including:

  • Monthly sales figures — even a simple spreadsheet is sufficient for most small operations.
  • Business expenses — ingredients, packaging, delivery costs, and marketing spend.
  • ACRA registration fees and equipment costs, which may be relevant for expense tracking.

GST registration is generally only required if your taxable turnover exceeds the prevailing GST registration threshold (currently S\$1 million) — a level far beyond what most home-based food businesses will reach, but worth knowing in case your business scales significantly.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming "no licence needed" means "no rules at all." SFA's food safety requirements still apply fully, even without a licence.
  • Selling ready-to-eat raw seafood, assuming it's just another product category — this is explicitly and strictly prohibited for home-based operations.
  • Hiring outside help to cook in your home kitchen — this breaks the Home-Based Business Scheme's core condition that only household members can be involved in production.
  • Selling at temporary fairs or markets — this is not allowed under the home-based food business framework, even if your regular home operation is otherwise compliant.
  • Not declaring income to IRAS — home-business income is taxable, and failing to keep records or declare it can create problems later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell baked goods from my HDB flat without any licence? Yes — small-scale home-based food businesses, including baked goods like cakes, cookies, and bread, generally do not require an SFA licence, provided you comply with SFA's food safety requirements and the Home-Based Business Scheme conditions.

Do I need to register my home-based food business with ACRA? Yes, if you're operating under a business name, selling regularly, or intending to make a profit in a sustained way — occasional one-off sales to friends may not trigger this requirement, but regular selling under a brand does.

Can I hire someone to help cook in my home-based food business? No — under the Home-Based Business Scheme, only people actually living in the flat or house can be involved in the business; you cannot bring in outside employees to work at your residence.

Can I sell raw seafood dishes from my home kitchen? No — home-based food businesses are strictly prohibited from selling ready-to-eat raw seafood, regardless of scale.

Do I need to pay tax on income from my home bakery? Yes — income from a home-based food business is taxable in Singapore, and you're expected to declare it to IRAS as part of your regular income reporting.

The Bottom Line

Starting a legal home-based food business in Singapore doesn't require navigating expensive licensing — but it does require carefully following SFA's food safety rules, staying within the Home-Based Business Scheme's conditions, registering with ACRA once you're operating as a genuine business, and properly declaring your income for tax purposes. Get these four pillars right, and your home bakery, cookie business, or bread-selling side project can run legally and sustainably from your own kitchen.

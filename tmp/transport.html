Singapore Transport Costs 2026: MRT, Bus, Grab and Car Ownership Compared

Singapore Transport Costs 2026: MRT, Bus, Grab and Car Ownership Compared

Quick Answer

  • Adult MRT/bus fares in 2026 are distance-based, ranging from S$1.09 to S$2.27 per ride with EZ-Link/registered SimplyGo payment.
  • The Adult Monthly Travel Card costs S$128/month; concessions include Senior (60+) at S$64, Polytechnic students at S$54, Tertiary students at S$58, and Persons with Disabilities at S$64.
  • HDB parking runs roughly S$0.60 per half-hour (S$1.20/hour) in central locations, with many estates free overnight from 10:30pm to 7am.
  • A full commuting workday by car in the CBD — parking plus ERP both ways — can cost around S$55/day, or roughly S$1,100/month, before fuel, insurance, depreciation, or the COE itself.
  • ERP 2.0, a satellite-based system replacing physical gantries, begins rolling out from late 2026, initially keeping zone-based charging before moving toward distance-based pricing.
  • For most short commutes under 3km, public transport costs as little as S$43/month — a fraction of car ownership costs.

Introduction

Deciding how to get around Singapore is one of the most consequential recurring cost decisions a resident makes, yet the comparison between MRT/bus, Grab, and car ownership is rarely laid out with real numbers in one place. Car owners often underestimate the combined weight of COE, ERP, parking, and depreciation, while public transport commuters may not realise whether a monthly pass actually saves them money given their specific commute pattern. This guide puts 2026 fare structures, parking rates, and motoring costs side by side so you can make an informed, numbers-based transport decision.

ERP and ERP 2.0

Current ERP rates range from about S$0.50 to S$6 per pass, varying by location, time band, and traffic conditions, with cars, taxis and PHVs charged separately from heavy vehicles. From late 2026, ERP 2.0 — a satellite-based system using new on-board units (OBUs) installed in every vehicle — begins taking over. Initially, charging remains zone-based, mirroring existing gantry locations and time bands, with rates staying in the same S$0.50–S$6 range. Over time, the system is expected to move toward distance-based charging in congestion zones, where drivers pay per kilometre driven in a charged area rather than per gantry crossing.

Motoring Payment Methods

The EZ-Link Motoring Card is compatible with dual-mode in-vehicle units (IUs) and can be used for ERP and carpark payments at all CEPAS-compliant carparks. Alternatively, the SimplyGo Motoring Service offers a postpaid, cardless option for ERP and carpark charges, managed via the SimplyGo app.

Common Mistakes Residents Make

  • Buying an Adult Monthly Travel Card without checking break-even trips: For shorter or infrequent commutes, per-ride fares can be cheaper than the flat S$128 monthly cost.
  • Underestimating true car ownership cost: Residents often calculate only fuel and parking, forgetting COE, road tax, insurance, and depreciation, which dominate total cost of ownership.
  • Ignoring free early-bird MRT/LRT windows: Commuters who could easily shift their schedule slightly miss out on free travel on selected NEL and Sengkang-Punggol LRT stations.
  • Not linking a SimplyGo-compatible card for rewards: Paying by unregistered card or cash means missing out on cashback or miles available through registered SimplyGo-compatible credit cards.
  • Assuming ERP 2.0 will immediately change costs: ERP 2.0's initial rollout in late 2026 keeps the same zone-based logic and rate range as today — the bigger changes (distance-based charging) come later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Adult Monthly Travel Card worth it for my commute? It depends on your trip distance and frequency — for many mid-to-long commutes done twice daily on weekdays only, per-ride fares can be cheaper than the flat S$128 monthly cost; the pass becomes more attractive with heavier or longer-distance usage, including weekends.

How much does ERP 2.0 cost compared to current ERP? Initially, ERP 2.0 keeps rates in the same S$0.50–S$6 range and remains zone-based; the shift toward distance-based, per-kilometre charging in congestion zones is expected to develop over time after the late 2026 rollout begins.

Can I use my EZ-Link Motoring Card for both driving and public transport? The EZ-Link Motoring Card is designed primarily for ERP and carpark payments; general EZ-Link cards or SimplyGo-registered bank cards are used for public transport fares.

Is it cheaper to drive or take public transport to the CBD? Based on 2026 figures, public transport is substantially cheaper for CBD commuting once parking and ERP costs are included, even before accounting for COE, insurance, and depreciation.

Do I need the SimplyGo app to use SimplyGo? No — the app's primary function is monitoring your travel history; you can tap contactless bank cards or mobile wallets directly without installing the app.

Are there free MRT rides available during peak hours? Free rides apply only on selected stations of the North East Line and Sengkang-Punggol LRT, before 7:30am and between 9:00am–9:45am on weekdays, not across the entire network.

Which credit cards are best for SimplyGo and public transport savings? Certain cards, such as those offering cashback on SimplyGo transactions, provide rewards on daily commuting spend; check current bank offers for cashback percentages and monthly caps before choosing one.

Key Takeaways

  • Public transport fares in 2026 range from S$1.09 to S$2.27 per ride, making it dramatically cheaper than car ownership for most commutes.
  • The Adult Monthly Travel Card at S$128 is only worthwhile above a certain trip-frequency break-even point — do the maths for your specific commute.
  • Car ownership costs go far beyond fuel and parking, with ERP, COE, insurance and depreciation forming the bulk of true monthly cost.
  • ERP 2.0's late-2026 rollout initially preserves current zone-based rates before shifting toward distance-based pricing over time.

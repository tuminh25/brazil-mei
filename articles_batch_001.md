# Complete Guide to HDB Resale Grants in 2026

## Quick Answer

- First-timer families buying an HDB resale flat can receive **up to S$230,000** across three CPF housing grants: the CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats, the Enhanced CPF Housing Grant (EHG), and the Proximity Housing Grant (PHG).
- First-timer singles buying alone can receive **up to S$115,000** in combined grants.
- The CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats gives Singapore Citizen (SC) couples up to **S$80,000** (2- to 4-room flats) or **S$50,000** (5-room or larger); SC-PR couples receive S$70,000 or S$40,000 respectively.
- EHG is income-tested: families earning S$1,500 or less a month can get up to **S$120,000**, sliding down to S$80,000 at S$3,501–S$4,000 household income, and phasing out above a S$9,000 household income ceiling.
- Grants are credited directly into your CPF Ordinary Account — no cash is disbursed to you.
- You must apply for a Housing and Financing Eligibility (HFE) letter before you can confirm which grants you qualify for.

## Introduction

Grants are the single biggest lever HDB resale buyers have to reduce their out-of-pocket cost, yet most residents only discover the full grant stack — CPF Housing Grant, EHG and PHG — after they've already started house hunting, by which time they may have anchored on a flat outside their real budget. Because grant amounts depend on household income, citizenship combination, flat size and proximity to parents, two families looking at the same flat can qualify for very different total support. This guide breaks down every resale grant available in 2026, shows how they stack, and walks through a real worked example so you know your numbers before you make an offer.

## What Grants Are Available for HDB Resale Flats

There are three main CPF housing grants for first-timer resale buyers in 2026.

### 1. CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats (Families and Singles)

This is the base grant for first-timer buyers purchasing a resale flat on the open market.

| Household Structure | 2- to 4-room resale flat | 5-room or larger resale flat |
|---|---|---|
| SC + SC couple | S$80,000 | S$50,000 |
| SC + PR couple | S$70,000 | S$40,000 |
| Single SC (buying alone) | S$40,000 | S$25,000 |
| Single + Single (joint singles purchase) | S$50,000 | S$40,000 |

Household income ceiling: S$14,000 for couples/families, S$21,000 if buying with extended family members such as parents.

### 2. Enhanced CPF Housing Grant (EHG)

EHG is available to first-timer applicants buying either a new BTO flat or a resale flat, and is layered on top of the base CPF Housing Grant for resale purchases.

| Average Monthly Household Income | EHG for First-Timer Families |
|---|---|
| ≤ S$1,500 | S$120,000 |
| S$1,501 – S$2,000 | S$110,000 |
| S$2,001 – S$2,500 | S$105,000 |
| S$2,501 – S$3,000 | S$95,000 |
| S$3,001 – S$3,500 | S$90,000 |
| S$3,501 – S$4,000 | S$80,000 |

The EHG household income ceiling is S$9,000 for families with at least two first-timer applicants. If applying as a first-timer with a second-timer, the ceiling is halved to S$4,500.

For singles, EHG (Singles) offers up to S$60,000, subject to a S$4,500 monthly income ceiling (or S$9,000 if buying with another single or with parents on a resale flat). You must also be employed continuously for 12 months before your flat application and remain employed when applying.

### 3. Proximity Housing Grant (PHG)

PHG rewards buyers who live with or near their parents or married children.

| Household | Living with parents/child | Living within 4km of parents/child |
|---|---|---|
| Married couples/families | S$30,000 | S$20,000 |
| Single citizens | S$15,000 | S$10,000 |

## How the Grants Stack: Maximum Totals

- **First-timer families:** CPF Housing Grant (up to S$80,000) + EHG (up to S$120,000) + PHG (up to S$30,000) = **up to S$230,000**.
- **First-timer singles:** CPF Housing Grant (up to S$40,000) + EHG Singles (up to S$60,000) + PHG Singles (up to S$15,000) = **up to S$115,000**.

These are ceiling figures — your actual amount depends entirely on your household income band, flat size, and proximity choice.

## Worked Example: Young Couple Buying Their First Resale Flat

**Profile:** A newly married SC-SC couple, combined monthly household income of S$4,800, buying a 4-room resale flat, and choosing to live within 4km of the wife's parents.

1. **CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats:** 4-room flat, SC-SC couple → S$80,000.
2. **EHG:** Household income of S$4,800 falls outside the table bands shown above (which cap out at S$4,000 for the highest listed tier), so the couple should check the full EHG income schedule up to the S$9,000 ceiling via their HFE letter — for illustration, assume a mid-range EHG amount applies given their income level.
3. **PHG:** Living within 4km of parents → S$20,000.

Combined, this couple is looking at CPF Housing Grant (S$80,000) + PHG (S$20,000), plus an EHG amount determined by their income band, illustrating how even a middle-income couple can access well over S$100,000 in grants credited straight into their CPF Ordinary Account to offset the flat price and reduce their loan quantum.

## BTO vs Resale Grants: Key Differences

| Factor | BTO Flat | Resale Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Grants available | EHG, Step-Up CPF Housing Grant | CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats, EHG, PHG |
| Total grant ceiling (families) | Up to S$120,000 (EHG only) | Up to S$230,000 (all three grants) |
| Waiting time | Typically 3–5 years | Immediate to a few months |
| Price certainty | Fixed at launch | Subject to market and Cash-Over-Valuation (COV) |
| Location choice | Limited to launch sites | Full island choice |

## Common Mistakes Residents Make

- **Assuming grant ceilings apply to everyone:** The advertised maximums (S$230,000 or S$115,000) only apply to the lowest income bands. Many households qualify for significantly less once their actual income is factored in.
- **Not applying for an HFE letter first:** Without an HFE assessment, you won't know your real grant eligibility or loan ceiling, and may end up making an offer you can't actually finance.
- **Forgetting the income ceiling cliff-edge:** Crossing from S$9,000 to S$9,001 in household income can disqualify you entirely from EHG — timing bonuses or salary increments around your application matters.
- **Ignoring PHG's 4km definition:** "Living near" parents is a specific 4km radius, not a subjective notion of "nearby" — always verify addresses before assuming eligibility.
- **Not accounting for Cash-Over-Valuation (COV):** Grants offset the flat price, but any COV must typically be paid in cash, not CPF, which can catch first-timers by surprise.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use both EHG and the CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats together?**
Yes. First-timer families and singles can combine the CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats, EHG, and PHG, subject to meeting each grant's individual eligibility criteria.

**Do grants get paid to me in cash?**
No. All CPF housing grants are credited directly into the recipient's CPF Ordinary Account and used to offset the purchase price or reduce your housing loan — they are never disbursed as cash.

**What happens if my household income is above S$9,000?**
You will not qualify for EHG, though you may still be eligible for the CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats if your income is below its S$14,000 (or S$21,000) ceiling.

**Is the PHG amount different if I live with my parents versus near them?**
Yes. Living with parents/child qualifies for a higher grant (S$30,000 for families) than living within 4km (S$20,000 for families).

**Can second-timers get any resale grants?**
Second-timers have access to a more limited set of grants, such as the Step-Up CPF Housing Grant in specific circumstances, but are generally not eligible for the first-timer CPF Housing Grant, EHG, or PHG at the same levels as first-timers.

**How do I find out exactly how much grant I qualify for?**
Apply for a Housing and Financing Eligibility (HFE) letter through HDB, which will assess your income, citizenship, and household structure to confirm your exact grant amounts.

**Do singles get lower grants than families?**
Yes. Singles buying alone generally receive roughly half the grant amount of a family/couple applying jointly, reflecting the lower household size.

## Key Takeaways

- Resale grants can total up to S$230,000 for families and S$115,000 for singles, but actual amounts depend heavily on household income.
- The three main grants — CPF Housing Grant for Resale Flats, EHG, and PHG — can be combined but each has its own income ceiling and eligibility rules.
- Always secure an HFE letter before house hunting so your grant expectations match reality.
- Grants are credited to CPF, not cash, and do not cover any Cash-Over-Valuation.

# CPF Changes in 2026: What Singapore Residents Need to Know

## Quick Answer

- The CPF Ordinary Wage (OW) ceiling rose to **S$8,000 per month** from January 2026 (up from S$7,400), the final step of the CPF strengthening plan that began in 2023.
- Combined employer (17%) and employee (20%) CPF contributions on the new ceiling total **S$2,960 per month** (S$1,360 employer + S$1,600 employee).
- Total annual CPF contributions (Ordinary + Additional Wages) are capped at **S$102,000**.
- CPF contribution rates for workers aged 55 to 65 rise by **1.5 percentage points** in 2026, flowing mainly into the Retirement Account.
- Employers get a one-year **CPF Transition Offset** in 2026 covering half of the increase in employer contributions.
- The MediSave withdrawal cap for outpatient scans doubles from S$300 to S$600 from 1 January 2026, and CareShield Life payouts now grow 4% a year (up from 2%).

## Introduction

CPF touches almost every financial decision a Singapore resident makes — from how much take-home pay you see each month, to how much you can use for housing, to how prepared you are for retirement and healthcare costs. 2026 brings one of the most significant rounds of CPF changes in recent years: a higher wage ceiling, adjusted contribution rates for older workers, and enhanced MediSave and CareShield Life provisions. Because these changes affect payslips, retirement planning, and healthcare budgeting simultaneously, understanding them properly — rather than picking up fragments from social media — is essential for financial planning in 2026 and beyond.

## The Key CPF Changes in 2026

### 1. Ordinary Wage (OW) Ceiling Increase

From January 2026, the OW ceiling — the maximum monthly salary on which CPF contributions are computed — rose from S$7,400 to S$8,000. This is the final step of a multi-year plan that began in 2023 to align the OW ceiling with rising incomes.

| Year | OW Ceiling |
|---|---|
| Before 2023 | S$6,000 |
| 2023 | S$6,300 |
| 2024 | S$6,800 |
| 2025 | S$7,400 |
| 2026 | S$8,000 |

### 2. Contribution Rates and Caps

| Party | Rate on OW | Max Monthly Contribution (at S$8,000 OW) |
|---|---|---|
| Employer | 17% | S$1,360 |
| Employee | 20% | S$1,600 |
| **Total** | **37%** | **S$2,960** |

Combining Ordinary Wage and Additional Wage (bonus) contributions, total CPF contributions are capped at **S$102,000 annually**. If annual ordinary wages do not exceed S$96,000 (12 × S$8,000), the Additional Wage Ceiling allows actual additional wages to be contributed; if ordinary wages exceed S$96,000, the additional wage ceiling is capped at S$6,000 (S$102,000 − S$96,000).

### 3. Higher Contribution Rates for Older Workers

CPF contribution rates for employees aged above 55 to 65 will rise by about 1.5 percentage points in 2026 (roughly 1.0 percentage point from the employee and 0.5 percentage point from the employer). The increase flows mainly into the Retirement Account, strengthening retirement adequacy for this age group. To cushion the cost for businesses, employers receive a one-year CPF Transition Offset in 2026, covering half of the increase in employer contributions.

### 4. Retirement and Re-Employment Age

Singapore's retirement age will rise from 63 to 64, and the re-employment age from 68 to 69, extending the period workers can remain formally employed with CPF contributions continuing.

### 5. Healthcare-Linked CPF Changes

- **CareShield Life** payouts will grow at 4% per year from 2026 (up from 2%), helping long-term care benefits keep pace with rising costs; the government has committed about S$570 million in additional subsidies to cushion premium increases.
- **MediSave withdrawal cap for outpatient scans** doubles from S$300 to S$600, effective 1 January 2026.
- **Flexi-MediSave** will, from mid-2026, cover restorative dental procedures such as permanent crowns and root canals for seniors aged 60 and above.
- A new **Matched MediSave Scheme (MMSS)** runs from 2026 to 2030, matching dollar-for-dollar voluntary cash top-ups to MediSave for eligible Singaporeans aged 55–70, up to S$1,000 per year.

### 6. CPF Top-Ups from Budget 2026

Eligible Singaporeans aged 50 and above in 2026 (born 1976 or earlier) will receive a CPF top-up of up to S$1,500 into their Retirement Account or Special Account in December 2026. The exact amount depends on existing retirement savings, with those holding between S$60,000 and just under the 2026 Basic Retirement Sum (S$110,200) receiving different tiered amounts.

## Worked Example: Mid-Career Employee Aged 57

**Profile:** An employee aged 57 earning a monthly Ordinary Wage of S$8,000, born in 1969 (so aged 57 in 2026, eligible for the age-50-and-above top-up cohort).

- **Before the 2026 increase:** Contributions were computed on a lower OW ceiling and a lower rate band for the 55–65 age group.
- **From 2026:** Contributions are now computed on the full S$8,000 OW ceiling, and the applicable employee/employer rates for the 55–65 band rise by about 1.5 percentage points combined, with most of that increase channelled into the Retirement Account rather than the Ordinary or Special Account.
- **Net effect:** Slightly lower take-home pay in exchange for materially stronger retirement savings, partly offset for the employer by the one-year Transition Offset.
- **Bonus:** If eligible under the Budget 2026 measures, this employee could also receive up to S$1,500 credited to their Retirement or Special Account in December 2026.

| Factor | Keeping funds in CPF Special/Retirement Account | Investing via CPFIS or cash brokerage |
|---|---|---|
| Guaranteed return | Yes — approximately 4% per year on CPF SA/RA | No — market-dependent |
| Risk | Very low (government-backed) | Variable, can lose principal |
| Liquidity | Locked until eligible withdrawal age/purpose | Generally more flexible |
| Suitability | Older members prioritising certainty | Younger members with longer horizon and risk tolerance |

## Common Mistakes Residents Make

- **Not checking which account top-ups land in:** The December 2026 top-up may go to either the Retirement Account or Special Account depending on individual circumstances — assuming it always goes to one specific account can lead to poor planning.
- **Ignoring the Additional Wage ceiling interaction:** Employees with large annual bonuses often miscalculate their total CPF contribution because they forget how the S$102,000 annual cap interacts with the Additional Wage Ceiling formula.
- **Overlooking the Transition Offset as a one-year measure:** Employers sometimes assume the CPF Transition Offset is permanent; it is only available for one year in 2026, so cost planning should account for its expiry.
- **Missing the raised MediSave outpatient scan cap:** Residents who assume the old S$300 cap still applies may under-claim for eligible outpatient scans after 1 January 2026.
- **Not planning around the retirement age shift:** Assuming retirement/re-employment ages are unchanged can affect both personal financial planning and HR policy compliance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the higher OW ceiling reduce my take-home pay?**
Yes, for employees earning above S$7,400 per month, a larger portion of your wage is now subject to CPF contributions, which slightly reduces take-home pay while increasing CPF savings.

**Who receives the CPF Transition Offset?**
Employers receive the one-year CPF Transition Offset in 2026, covering half of the increase in employer contributions for workers aged 55 to 65 — it is not paid directly to employees.

**How much is the December 2026 CPF top-up?**
Eligible Singaporeans aged 50 and above (born 1976 or earlier) can receive up to S$1,500, credited to their Retirement Account or Special Account, with the exact amount depending on their existing retirement savings.

**Can I still withdraw more than S$300 for outpatient scans under the old cap?**
From 1 January 2026, the annual MediSave withdrawal cap for outpatient scans doubles to S$600, so eligible residents can withdraw more than the previous S$300 limit.

**What is the Matched MediSave Scheme (MMSS)?**
MMSS runs from 2026 to 2030 and matches dollar-for-dollar voluntary cash top-ups to MediSave for eligible Singaporeans aged 55 to 70, up to S$1,000 per year.

**Will the retirement age change affect my current job?**
The retirement age is rising from 63 to 64 and the re-employment age from 68 to 69, meaning employers must offer continued employment or re-employment for a longer period before residents reach the new statutory ages.

**Does CareShield Life become more expensive in 2026?**
Payouts are increasing (4% annual growth instead of 2%), and while this can mean higher long-term premiums, the government has committed about S$570 million in additional subsidies to cushion the impact.

## Key Takeaways

- The 2026 OW ceiling increase to S$8,000 is the final step of a multi-year CPF strengthening plan.
- Workers aged 55–65 see higher contribution rates in 2026, mostly benefiting the Retirement Account, with employers cushioned by a one-year Transition Offset.
- Healthcare-linked CPF enhancements — higher MediSave scan caps, Flexi-MediSave dental coverage, and the new Matched MediSave Scheme — expand what MediSave can be used for.
- Eligible residents aged 50+ can receive a CPF top-up of up to S$1,500 in December 2026.

# CHAS, MediSave and MediShield Life: How Singapore Healthcare Financing Really Works

## Quick Answer

- Singapore's healthcare financing system rests on the **"3 Ms"**: MediSave (compulsory medical savings), MediShield Life (basic national health insurance), and Medifund (payer of last resort for those who still cannot afford care).
- **CHAS** (Community Health Assist Scheme) provides subsidies at private GP and dental clinics, split into **Green, Orange and Blue** tiers based on household income per person and the Annual Value (AV) of your home.
- MediSave contributions come from 8–10% of monthly wages for citizens and PRs, held on your behalf and usable only for approved medical purposes.
- MediShield Life is a basic health insurance plan that all Singaporeans and PRs are automatically enrolled in, with premiums paid from MediSave.
- Integrated Shield Plans (IPs) are private add-ons that must include MediShield Life as their base layer.
- What you actually pay depends on your CHAS tier, MediSave balance, birth cohort (Pioneer/Merdeka Generation), and whether you hold an Integrated Shield Plan.

## Introduction

Ask most Singapore residents to explain exactly how their last clinic bill was subsidised, and many will struggle — not because the system is poorly designed, but because it is genuinely a patchwork of interacting schemes. A 2024 MediShield Life Review found that most residents had heard of MediShield Life but did not understand its actual parameters, and confusion about CHAS tiers and MediSave eligibility remains one of the most common healthcare frustrations residents raise online. This guide untangles the "3 Ms" and CHAS system into one coherent picture, so you know exactly what each scheme covers before your next medical bill arrives.

## The Three Pillars: MediSave, MediShield Life, Medifund

### MediSave

MediSave is a national medical savings scheme. Singapore Citizens and PRs are required to contribute a portion of their monthly wages — typically in the 8–10% range depending on age — into a personal MediSave account. This money is held by the government and can only be used for approved medical purposes: hospitalisation, day surgery, and specific outpatient expenses such as chronic disease management, vaccinations, and now (from 2026) higher caps for outpatient scans and select dental procedures.

To use MediSave at a private institution, you generally need to submit a Medical Claims Authorisation Form (MCAF) — either a one-time version (MCAF(M)) for ongoing treatment authorisation, or a per-visit version (MCAF(S)).

### MediShield Life

MediShield Life is Singapore's basic health insurance plan. All Singapore Citizens and PRs are automatically enrolled, and premiums are paid using MediSave funds. It is designed to help with larger, more expensive hospital bills and serious illnesses rather than everyday outpatient visits. Integrated Shield Plans (IPs), offered by private insurers, must incorporate MediShield Life as their foundational layer, then add private hospital or higher-class ward coverage on top.

### Medifund

Medifund is the payment method of last resort for Singaporeans and PRs who genuinely cannot afford their medical bills even after MediSave and MediShield Life payouts have been applied.

## CHAS: Green, Orange and Blue Explained

CHAS lets Singapore Citizens (including Pioneer Generation and Merdeka Generation cardholders) receive subsidies for medical and dental care at participating private GP and dental clinics — but not at polyclinics, where subsidies work differently.

### Eligibility Criteria

| Criteria | CHAS Green | CHAS Orange | CHAS Blue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Household monthly income per person | Above S$2,300 | S$1,501 – S$2,300 | S$1,500 and below |
| Annual Value (AV) of residence (no income) | Above S$31,000 | S$21,001 – S$31,000 | S$21,000 and below |

### Subsidy Amounts

| Condition | CHAS Green | CHAS Orange | CHAS Blue | Merdeka Generation | Pioneer Generation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common illnesses (e.g. cough, cold) | Not applicable | Up to S$10/visit | Up to S$18.50/visit | Up to S$23.50/visit | Up to S$28.50/visit |
| Simple chronic conditions | Up to S$28/visit, capped S$112/year | Up to S$50/visit, capped S$200/year | Up to S$80/visit, capped S$320/year | Up to S$85/visit, capped S$340/year | Up to S$90/visit, capped S$360/year |
| Complex chronic conditions | Up to S$40/visit, capped S$160/year | Up to S$80/visit, capped S$320/year | Up to S$125/visit, capped S$500/year | Up to S$130/visit, capped S$520/year | Up to S$135/visit, capped S$540/year |

## Worked Example: A Family Navigating a Chronic Condition

**Profile:** A household with per-person monthly income of S$1,800, qualifying for CHAS Orange, where one member has a diagnosed simple chronic condition requiring regular GP visits.

- At each visit, the member receives up to **S$50 in subsidy**, capped at **S$200 per year** under CHAS Orange for simple chronic conditions.
- If the same household includes a Merdeka Generation grandparent with the same condition, that grandparent instead receives up to **S$85 per visit, capped at S$340 per year** — nearly double the CHAS Orange subsidy — reflecting the additional Merdeka Generation healthcare benefits layered on top of the base CHAS tier.
- Any remaining out-of-pocket cost at the GP can potentially be offset using MediSave if the visit and treatment fall under an approved MediSave use case, and if hospitalisation is later required, MediShield Life (or an Integrated Shield Plan, if held) would apply to the hospital bill.

## MediShield Life vs Integrated Shield Plan

| Factor | MediShield Life | Integrated Shield Plan (IP) |
|---|---|---|
| Enrolment | Automatic for all SC/PR | Optional, purchased from private insurer |
| Coverage | Basic hospitalisation, B2/C class wards | Enhanced coverage, private/higher-class wards |
| Premium source | Paid via MediSave | Base premium via MediSave; IP rider often paid in cash |
| Cost | Lower | Higher, scales with age and rider tier |
| Best for | Baseline protection for all residents | Residents wanting private hospital access or larger ward upgrades |

## Common Mistakes Residents Make

- **Assuming CHAS covers polyclinic visits:** CHAS subsidies apply only at participating private GP and dental clinics, not polyclinics, which use a separate subsidy structure.
- **Not knowing your own CHAS tier:** Many residents never check whether they qualify for Green, Orange or Blue, and miss out on subsidies they are entitled to.
- **Confusing MediShield Life with an Integrated Shield Plan:** Some residents believe they are only covered by their IP and forget that MediShield Life is the mandatory base layer underneath it.
- **Not submitting the MCAF form correctly:** Failing to authorise MediSave withdrawal in advance (via MCAF(M)) can mean unnecessary manual claims for every single visit.
- **Overlooking Pioneer/Merdeka Generation enhancements:** Eligible seniors sometimes continue paying full CHAS Orange/Blue rates without realising their generation cohort entitles them to higher subsidies.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use MediSave to pay at a CHAS clinic?**
Yes, for MediSave-accredited GP and dental clinics, MediSave can be used for approved treatments in addition to any CHAS subsidy you receive, subject to MediSave withdrawal rules and caps.

**What is the difference between CHAS Green and having no CHAS card?**
CHAS Green does not provide a common illness subsidy but does provide chronic disease management subsidies, making it still valuable for residents managing ongoing conditions, unlike having no CHAS card at all.

**Why did the hospital tell me something different from what CPF Board said about my MediShield Life claim?**
Hospitals administer your bill and initial subsidy calculations, but the CPF Board handles MediShield Life claim-specific queries — for detailed payout questions, you generally need to contact the CPF Board directly rather than the hospital.

**Do Pioneer Generation members get more than Merdeka Generation members?**
Yes. Pioneer Generation subsidies are generally higher across common illness and chronic condition categories compared to Merdeka Generation, reflecting their earlier package structure.

**Is MediShield Life enough on its own, or do I need an Integrated Shield Plan?**
MediShield Life provides baseline hospitalisation coverage for B2/C class wards; residents wanting private hospital access or higher ward classes typically need an Integrated Shield Plan as a top-up.

**How is my CHAS tier determined if I have no income?**
If your household has no income, your CHAS tier is instead determined by the Annual Value (AV) of your residence.

**Can foreigners or non-PR residents get CHAS subsidies?**
CHAS is available to Singapore Citizens, including Pioneer and Merdeka Generation cardholders; it does not extend equivalent subsidies to non-citizen, non-PR residents.

## Key Takeaways

- Singapore's healthcare financing model layers MediSave, MediShield Life, and Medifund, with CHAS providing additional subsidies at private clinics.
- CHAS tiers (Green, Orange, Blue) and Pioneer/Merdeka Generation status materially change how much subsidy you receive per visit.
- MediShield Life is your automatic baseline; an Integrated Shield Plan is an optional upgrade, not a replacement.
- Knowing your CHAS tier and submitting the right MediSave authorisation forms in advance prevents overpaying at each visit.

## What Is the SkillsFuture Level-Up Programme?
